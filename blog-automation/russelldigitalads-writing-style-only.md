# RussellDigitalAds.com Writing Style Guide

## Purpose

Use this guide to write RussellDigitalAds.com articles in a direct, practical, human style.

The writing should sound like an experienced SEO and Google Ads provider explaining a real lead-generation problem to a busy local business owner.

It should not sound like:

- A generic marketing agency
- An SEO textbook
- A polished corporate brochure
- A software company
- An AI-generated summary
- A long sales pitch

The article should help the reader understand what is wrong, what matters, and what should be done next.

---

# Core Voice

## The Voice in One Sentence

A practical search-marketing expert explaining SEO, Google Ads, websites, and lead generation in plain English.

## Voice Traits

The writing should be:

- Direct
- Helpful
- Practical
- Confident without sounding arrogant
- Slightly informal
- Easy to scan
- Clear enough for a business owner who does not know marketing terminology
- Focused on calls, forms, estimate requests, quote requests, and booked jobs
- Specific about what should change
- Commercial without constantly selling

The writing should not be:

- Corporate
- Academic
- Overly polished
- Dramatic
- Filled with jargon
- Full of metaphors
- Repetitive
- Overly enthusiastic
- Perfectly symmetrical
- Written like every sentence was carefully optimized

---

# Audience

Write for small and medium-sized local businesses, especially service businesses.

Examples include:

- Electricians
- HVAC companies
- Plumbers
- Roofers
- Landscapers
- Contractors
- Cleaning companies
- Auto repair shops
- Home-service businesses
- Other companies that depend on phone calls, estimate requests, and booked jobs

The reader usually wants a practical answer to a question such as:

- Why is my website not generating calls?
- Why do competitors rank above me?
- Do I need SEO or Google Ads?
- Why are my Google Ads leads bad?
- What should an SEO company actually do?
- Do I need more service pages?
- How do I rank in Google Maps?
- How do I know which searches produce leads?
- What should I fix first?

Answer the real question early.

Do not spend the first 200 words explaining the history of SEO, digital marketing, or Google.

---

# Main Writing Goal

The writing should connect marketing work to what the business actually wants.

The real goals are usually:

- More qualified phone calls
- More estimate requests
- More quote forms
- More booked jobs
- Better lead quality
- A clearer understanding of where leads came from

Traffic, impressions, clicks, and rankings can matter, but they are not the final goal.

Do not treat “more traffic” as the main outcome unless the article is specifically about traffic.

---

# Sentence Style

Use mostly short and medium-length sentences.

Longer sentences are fine when they carry useful detail, but do not stack several abstract ideas together.

Preferred:

> A service page should target one clear job and one clear search intent. When every service is packed onto the homepage, Google has less context and the customer has to work harder to find what they need.

Avoid:

> In today’s rapidly evolving digital ecosystem, local businesses must leverage comprehensive omnichannel optimization frameworks to maximize visibility and achieve scalable growth.

## Sentence Habits to Use

- Start with the answer, problem, or action.
- Use concrete words such as call, form, estimate, page, keyword, ad, budget, service, and customer.
- Say what should actually change.
- Use contractions when they sound natural.
- Use first-person experience sparingly.
- Let some sections end directly.
- Prefer one normal sentence over a polished three-part sentence.
- Make transitions feel natural instead of announcing every shift.
- Use examples from real service businesses when useful.
- Vary sentence length.
- Allow occasional repetition when it helps clarity.

## Sentence Habits to Avoid

- Starting every paragraph with a transition
- Using “Additionally,” “Furthermore,” or “Moreover”
- Writing every sentence at the same length
- Repeating the article title in different words
- Grouping three benefits into every sentence
- Explaining obvious points just to add length
- Ending every section with a neat summary
- Making every sentence sound polished and complete

---

# Paragraph Style

Keep most paragraphs between one and three sentences.

A one-sentence paragraph is fine when the point matters.

Aim for roughly 25 to 65 words per paragraph.

Do not create large blocks of text.

Do not split every sentence into its own paragraph either.

## Good Paragraph Rhythm

A normal section may look like this:

- One short opening paragraph
- One slightly longer explanation
- A useful list
- One short closing point

Not every section needs the same structure.

Some sections can be short.

Some can carry more detail.

The article should feel naturally uneven instead of perfectly balanced.

## Density Rule

Do not place more than three normal paragraphs in a row without using one of these:

- A useful subheading
- Bullets
- Numbered steps
- A bold-label list
- A short example

Break up the text before it becomes tiring to read.

---

# Headings

Use headings that tell the reader exactly what the section covers.

Good headings:

- Why Your Website Gets Traffic but No Calls
- Build a Page for Each Service You Want to Sell
- What Google Maps Needs From a Local Business
- Stop Paying for Searches That Will Never Convert
- Track Calls and Forms Before Increasing the Budget
- What an SEO Agency Should Fix First
- When Google Ads Makes More Sense Than SEO
- How Electricians Can Rank for Higher-Value Jobs
- Common Local SEO Problems on Contractor Websites
- What to Look for in an SEO Company

Avoid vague or clever headings:

- The Road Ahead
- Unlocking the Potential
- A New Era of Search
- The Bigger Picture
- Marketing Reimagined
- Everything Comes Together
- Your Path to Success
- The Future of Digital Marketing

## Heading Density

Use enough headings to keep the article easy to scan.

For a normal 750 to 1,000 word article, five to seven main sections usually works well.

Use subheadings when a section becomes dense or covers more than one clear point.

Do not force a heading every two sentences.

Do not make every heading follow the same grammatical pattern.

---

# Bullets and Numbered Lists

Use bullets when the reader is comparing several items.

Good uses for bullets:

- SEO problems
- Page requirements
- Keyword groups
- Google Ads checks
- Tracking needs
- Common mistakes
- Questions to ask an agency
- Reasons a page does not convert
- Content gaps
- Local SEO tasks
- Business-owner decisions

Use numbered lists only when the steps need to happen in order.

## Bold-Label Bullets

Bold-label bullets are useful when each item needs a short explanation.

Example:

- **Service pages:** Build a separate page for each important job the business wants more of.
- **Location pages:** Use these only for areas the company can actually serve.
- **Google Business Profile:** Keep the categories, hours, services, and contact details accurate.
- **Tracking:** Measure calls and forms before deciding whether the campaign is working.

Do not make every article a list.

Do not make every bullet the same length.

Do not force bold labels when normal bullets would be easier to read.

---

# Article Opening Style

Start with the real problem, decision, or useful fact.

Good opening habits:

- State the problem quickly.
- Name the industry, location, or service when the article is specific.
- Explain what the business is missing without exaggerating.
- Move into the article without announcing an outline.
- Use a different opening shape from article to article.

Example:

> An electrician can rank for the company name and still get almost no calls from Google. That usually means the website is visible only to people who already know the business.
>
> Local SEO is about showing up for the jobs people actually search for, such as panel upgrades, generator installation, and emergency electrical repair.

Avoid openings such as:

- Digital marketing is important for every business.
- In today’s competitive market...
- This article explains everything you need to know...
- In this guide, we will cover...
- Local businesses face many challenges...
- SEO can help businesses grow their online presence.
- In the modern digital landscape...

Do not begin with broad statistics unless they are truly needed.

---

# Human Writing Rules

The article should feel like it was written by someone who has reviewed local-business websites, rebuilt service pages, managed search campaigns, and explained the work to business owners.

Human writing comes from judgment and specificity, not random mistakes.

Use these habits:

- Mix short statements with a few fuller paragraphs.
- Make some transitions implicit.
- Use plain observations.
- Mention the actual page, search, call, form, service, or campaign.
- Allow a little repetition when the key point needs to stay clear.
- Use contractions when they sound normal.
- Let list items vary in length.
- End some sections without a summary sentence.
- Use specific examples when they help.
- Keep some wording slightly informal.

Examples of natural wording:

- This is where a lot of local sites lose the lead.
- A homepage cannot do the job of ten service pages.
- The ad may be getting clicks, but the search terms tell you whether those clicks are useful.
- More traffic does not help much when the page gives the visitor no reason to call.
- The business may not need more content yet. It may need better pages for the services it already sells.
- A monthly report should help the owner decide what to do next.

Do not intentionally add spelling mistakes.

Do not create random grammar errors to beat AI detectors.

The writing should feel human because it is plain, specific, useful, and slightly uneven.

- skyrocket your rankings

- your website is your digital storefront

Bad:

Better:

# Anti-Template Rules

Do not make every article follow the same pattern.

Avoid automatically using:

1. Definition
2. Benefits
3. Process
4. Common mistakes
5. FAQ
6. Conclusion

Use only the sections the topic needs.

Do not:

- Reuse the same opening
- Define SEO in every article
- Add a summary section by default
- Add FAQs only to increase length
- Use a large sales section at the end
- Repeat the same SEO checklist in every article
- Make every numbered step begin with a bold label
- Write neat groups of three unless the information naturally comes in three
- Force SEO and Google Ads into every article
- Force Russell Digital into every section
- End every section by saying it leads to more customers
- Use the same examples in every industry article
- Replace only the city or industry name in an otherwise identical article

Before finishing, ask:

> Could this wording be pasted into ten other agency articles by changing the business type?

When the answer is yes, rewrite it with the actual service, city, page, keyword, lead problem, or campaign issue.

# Technical Language

Use plain English first.

Explain jargon when it is needed.

Preferred:

> Search intent is the reason behind the search. Someone looking for “how to reset a breaker” is not as close to hiring an electrician as someone searching for “emergency electrician near me.”

Avoid:

> Search intent segmentation allows brands to align content assets with funnel-stage user behavior.

Use technical terms only when they help the reader make a decision.

Do not overuse:

- Topical authority
- Entity optimization
- Semantic relevance
- Funnel
- Omnichannel
- SERP features
- E-E-A-T
- AEO
- GEO
- Programmatic SEO
- Conversion architecture

When a technical term is necessary, explain it in one normal sentence.

---

# Sales Tone

Educate first.

Sell second.

The article should give the reader something useful even if they never contact Russell Digital.

Good:

> A roofer does not need one page that lists every service. Separate pages for roof repair, replacement, and storm damage give Google more context and give the visitor a clearer next step.

Avoid:

> Russell Digital offers unmatched SEO solutions that help roofing companies dominate search results and generate explosive growth.

Use practical first-person language sparingly:

- We review the pages that should be bringing in leads.
- We build content around the services customers are searching for.
- We fix tracking before increasing the ad budget.
- We explain what changed and what needs attention next.
- We use Google Ads when the business needs faster feedback or lead flow.

Do not repeat the company name in every section.

Do not use:

- our expert team
- industry-leading agency
- unmatched service
- best-in-class SEO
- world-class experience
- guaranteed growth
- guaranteed rankings

# Structure by Topic

## SEO Article

A useful SEO article may include:

- The actual ranking or lead problem
- What should be fixed first
- Service pages
- Local SEO
- Content
- Technical issues
- Tracking
- Common mistakes
- A short final takeaway

Use only the sections the topic needs.

## Google Ads Article

A useful Google Ads article may include:

- The paid-search problem
- Search intent
- Keyword selection
- Negative keywords
- Campaign or ad-group structure
- Landing-page fit
- Conversion tracking
- Budget decisions
- Lead quality

Do not make the article sound like Google Ads always works immediately.

## Industry-Specific Article

A useful industry article should include real examples from that business type.

It may cover:

- High-value services
- Common searches
- Required service pages
- Local competition
- Google Business Profile
- Reviews
- Tracking
- Lead quality

Do not swap only the business type into a generic article.

## Problem/Solution Article

A useful problem article should:

- State the problem
- Explain likely causes
- Show what to check
- Explain what should change
- Describe what to measure

Do not use fear-based language.

## Comparison Article

A useful comparison should explain:

- When each option makes sense
- What each one requires
- Speed and commitment
- Measurement
- Questions to ask
- Red flags

Do not force a winner when the answer depends on the business.

---

# FAQ Style

Use FAQs only when they add something new.

Usually two to four questions are enough.

Keep answers direct.

Do not repeat full sections in FAQ form.

Good FAQ questions:

- How long does local SEO take?
- Should a local business use SEO or Google Ads?
- Does every service need its own page?
- How do you track calls from Google?
- Can Google Ads work without a dedicated landing page?
- How many location pages should a business create?

Do not add FAQs only to use keywords.

---

# Final Style Check

Before finishing an article, confirm:

- The opening gets to the real problem quickly.
- The writing sounds like a person, not an agency template.
- Most paragraphs are short and easy to read.
- Headings are specific.
- Dense sections are broken up.
- Bullets are used where they help.
- Numbered steps are used only when order matters.
- The article uses actual business, service, page, keyword, or campaign examples.
- Traffic is not treated as the final goal.
- No unsupported promises appear.
- No generic AI or agency phrases appear.
- The article does not repeat the same point in several sections.
- The article does not feel perfectly balanced.
- The wording could not be pasted into ten other agency articles without major changes.
- The article educates more than it sells.
- The article is clear enough for a general local-business owner.

# Approved Style Reference

The following excerpt shows the approved general direction.

Do not copy it word for word into other articles.

Use it to understand pacing, directness, heading density, paragraph length, bullets, and practical tone.

## Local SEO for Service Businesses That Need More Calls

A local business can rank for its own name and still get almost no work from Google.

That usually means the website is visible only to people who already know the company. The real opportunity is showing up when someone searches for the service itself: electrical panel replacement, AC repair, drain cleaning, roof repair, or another job the business wants to book.

Local SEO connects those searches with the right page, the right location, and a clear way to call or request an estimate.

### Start With the Services That Make Money

A lot of local websites put every service into one long homepage section. It may tell visitors what the company does, but it gives Google very little detail about each job.

Start with the services the business actually wants more of.

- **Electricians:** Panel upgrades, generator installation, EV chargers, and emergency repairs may need separate pages.
- **HVAC companies:** AC repair, replacement, heating, and maintenance should not be treated as one keyword.
- **Plumbers:** Drain cleaning, water heaters, leak detection, and sewer work attract different searches.
- **Roofers:** Repair, replacement, storm damage, and commercial roofing are not the same buying decision.

Each important service page should explain the job, who needs it, the area served, and what the customer should do next.

### Fix the Google Business Profile

The website is only part of local search.

A complete Google Business Profile helps the business appear in Google Maps and other local results. The profile information should agree with the website, and the selected categories should reflect the work the company actually performs.

The basics matter:

- Correct business name, phone number, and website
- Accurate service area or address
- Relevant primary and secondary categories
- Current hours
- Real project and team photos
- A steady process for asking customers for reviews
- Useful responses to positive and negative reviews

Do not stuff the business name with keywords or create fake locations.

### Build Pages Around Real Searches

Keyword research should lead to pages, not a spreadsheet that nobody uses.

A useful keyword has three parts:

- **Service:** What the customer needs
- **Location:** Where the customer needs it
- **Intent:** Whether the person is looking for information or ready to hire

“Electrical services” is broad. “Emergency electrician in Houston” is much closer to a real lead.

The website does not need hundreds of thin pages with the city name swapped out. It needs useful pages for the services and areas the company can genuinely support.

### Track Calls and Forms

Rankings can improve while the business still gets poor leads.

That is why the tracking needs to follow the full path:

1. Which search brought the visitor in
2. Which page they landed on
3. Whether they called or completed a form
4. Whether the inquiry matched the service area and job type
5. Whether the lead became an estimate or customer

A monthly report should help the owner decide what to fix next. More charts are not the same as more clarity.

### Use Google Ads When Speed Matters

SEO takes time because the site has to build relevance and authority.

Google Ads can put the business in front of high-intent searches faster, but only when the campaign and landing page are built around the same service.

An ad for AC repair should not send the visitor to a homepage that talks equally about heating, plumbing, and electrical work. The customer searched for one thing. The page should make that next step obvious.

Paid search is also useful for testing. A business can learn which keywords produce calls before investing months into a larger organic content plan.

### Keep the Goal Simple

The goal is not to rank for the most keywords.

The goal is to show up for the right searches, send people to a page that answers the question, and make it easy to call or request an estimate.

Traffic matters. Rankings matter. But they only matter when they help the business create real opportunities.
