# Russell Digital Article Instructions

## Purpose

Use this as the controlling writing brief for Russell Digital blog posts, SEO pages, guides, and articles.

The article should sound like a practical SEO operator explaining what matters to a business owner who wants more qualified leads from search. Keep it direct, useful, and grounded in what Russell Digital actually says on russelldigitalads.com.

Do not write like a generic marketing agency. Do not write long blog-prose sections. Do not use AfricaCTN facts, shipping language, CTN terminology, port language, country pages, certificates, customs rules, or any logistics framing.

## Source Rules

Use only:

- Facts supplied directly by the topic brief
- Existing Russell Digital pages on russelldigitalads.com
- Existing Russell Digital blog posts for voice, internal links, and approved offer context
- The local topic queue notes when they narrow the angle

Do not use:

- Competitor websites
- External articles, tools, studies, statistics, or quotes
- Unsupported claims about rankings, revenue, conversion rates, traffic lifts, guarantees, certifications, partnerships, or proprietary data
- General marketing claims that are not backed by Russell Digital site content

If a fact is not on Russell Digital or in the topic brief, leave it out.

## Voice

Write like a hands-on SEO consultant talking to a busy owner:

- Structure the article like an AI Overview expanded into a practical guide
- Start with the answer
- Use a compact "Quick Answer" or answer-first section near the top
- Be plain and specific
- Use short sentences by default
- Explain cause and effect
- Say what to fix before explaining why
- Use examples a local business would recognize
- Sound confident without hype
- Keep paragraphs very tight
- Let the article feel slightly uneven and human

Do not use:

- "In today's digital landscape"
- "Unlock"
- "Leverage"
- "Game-changing"
- "Robust"
- "Seamless"
- "Skyrocket"
- "Dominate"
- "Revolutionary"
- "Delve"
- "Furthermore"
- "In conclusion"
- "Guaranteed rankings"
- "#1 SEO agency"

## Reader

Write for business owners, operators, and marketing managers who want search to produce real inquiries. They usually need to know:

- Whether SEO is worth doing
- What should be fixed first
- What a good SEO partner should actually deliver
- How to judge progress without vanity metrics
- Why their current website is not ranking or converting
- When to use SEO, PPC, content, local SEO, or technical cleanup

Assume they are smart but busy. Do not bury the answer.

## Article Shape

Default target: 650 to 900 words. Go longer only when every added section is useful.

Use:

- A direct opening of three to five very short paragraphs
- A "Quick Answer" section near the top when it fits
- Six to nine useful H2 sections
- H3s only when a subsection truly needs them
- Bullets for checks, mistakes, deliverables, red flags, and examples
- Numbered steps only when the order matters
- A short CTA near the end that points to a valid Russell Digital internal URL

Do not force a FAQ. Add one only when it answers useful search questions not already handled in the body.

## AI Overview Shape

Most articles should open like this:

1. One short answer sentence.
2. One or two short explanation paragraphs.
3. A compact bullet list that answers the main query.
4. Then move into short H2 sections.

Example rhythm:

```markdown
Focused service pages help local businesses attract better leads because they match one specific search intent.

That is the short answer.

A homepage has to explain the whole business. A focused service page only has to explain one service, one customer need, and one next step.

## Quick Answer

A good service page should tell the visitor:

- what the service is
- where it is available
- who it is for
- what is included
- what to do next
```

Do not open with a long introduction. Do not announce the article. Do not write "In this guide."

## Paragraph Rules

- Most paragraphs should be one or two sentences.
- Three sentences is okay when the idea needs it.
- Avoid long paragraphs unless there is a clear reason.
- Put a heading before the reader starts wondering what the section is about.
- Use bullets when a paragraph starts listing checks, mistakes, deliverables, or signals.
- Never jam multiple numbered steps into one paragraph.

## Internal Links

Every article must include at least one useful internal link.

Prefer links to:

- `/free-strategy-call-offer/`
- `/services/`
- `/pricing/`
- Relevant Russell Digital blog posts

Use internal links naturally. Do not stuff them. Do not link externally.

## Russell Digital Positioning

When relevant and supported by site content, use these ideas:

- Month-to-month SEO instead of long contract lock-in
- Clear monthly deliverables
- SEO work tied to leads, calls, conversions, and business outcomes
- Practical audits before big retainers
- Service pages, local SEO, technical SEO, content, internal linking, and reporting
- Strategy calls as the next step

Do not over-sell Russell Digital in every section. Educate first. Mention Russell Digital where it helps the reader understand the next move.

## Structure Patterns

Good section types:

- What this should include
- What to check before signing up
- What weak agencies skip
- What to measure
- What to fix first
- Red flags
- A simple decision framework
- Where Russell Digital fits

Good bullet style:

- **Service pages:** Explain what should be improved and why it affects leads.
- **Technical cleanup:** Mention crawlability, speed, indexation, schema, or broken pages only when relevant.
- **Reporting:** Focus on calls, forms, organic sessions, rankings, and visible work completed.

Keep labels short. Put the useful explanation after the colon.

## Claims and Safety

Do not claim:

- Guaranteed rankings
- Guaranteed leads
- Guaranteed revenue
- Specific percentages
- Specific prices unless the site or topic brief explicitly provides them
- Official partnerships or certifications
- That Russell Digital is the best, biggest, fastest, cheapest, or number one

Use measured language:

- "can help"
- "usually"
- "often"
- "a good sign"
- "a red flag"
- "worth checking"
- "the next practical step"

## Ending

End with a short, useful next step. Do not write a huge sales block.

Good:

If you want help deciding what should happen first, start with a [free strategy call](/free-strategy-call-offer/) and use it to pressure-test the SEO opportunity.

Bad:

Contact the premier digital marketing experts at Russell Digital today to unlock explosive growth.

## Final Check

Before finishing, remove:

- Competitor mentions
- External links
- Unsupported numbers
- Hype
- Repeated sales pitches
- Generic filler
- AfricaCTN or shipping language
- Long walls of text

Be useful first. Be direct. Keep it Russell Digital only.
