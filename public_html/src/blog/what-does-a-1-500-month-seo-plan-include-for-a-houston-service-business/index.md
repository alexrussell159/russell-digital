What a 3–5 article package looks like (practical, month-by-month)

This is an approved, transparent example of the monthly deliverable mix you can expect when a 3–5 article/package approach is the right fit for the market and the business.

Month 1 — Foundations and high-value pages

- **Publication:** 1 service page (or major rewrite) that targets the single highest-value job you want more of.
- **Tracking:** Conversion setup for calls and form submissions tied to that page.
- **Research deliverable:** Competitor gap report that explains why that page should win.

Month 2 — Local reach and supporting content

- **Publication:** 1 location page or nearby-area page focused on a real service area you serve.
- **On-page work:** Add local schema basics, clear call-to-action, and phone number prominence.
- **Internal link:** Connect the location page to the primary service page to pass relevance.

Month 3 — Supporting authority and intent coverage

- **Publication:** 1–2 supporting articles that answer common buyer questions or cover narrower service variants (example: emergency vs. scheduled repair).
- **Conversion focus:** Each article includes a clear next step: call, estimate request, or booking link.

Ongoing months — Repeat or reallocate

- **Publication:** 0–2 pages per month depending on measured impact and new opportunities.
- **Optimization:** Rewrites or A/B changes to pages that are getting impressions but not calls.

Why this mix works for service businesses in Houston

- A homepage cannot replace a dedicated service page for a single, high-value job. A separate page makes the buying decision obvious.
- Location pages matter only for areas you can actually serve. We prioritize real coverage, not a long list of copied city pages.
- Supporting articles capture related searches that show purchase intent or lead to the primary service pages.

A transparent decision framework: How we pick the 3–5 items

- Step 1 — Revenue-first filter: Pick services that create the most profitable jobs (repairs, replacements, emergency calls, or estimate-heavy work).
- Step 2 — Search-gap filter: Target searches your competitors rank for but you don’t.
- Step 3 — Intent filter: Prefer pages where intent is high (someone looking to hire) over purely informational queries.
- Step 4 — Capacity filter: Only add pages for services and areas you can actually support this quarter.

Sample deliverable: What you get for a single service page

- Title and meta targeted to the specific search intent.
- A one-page outline that answers who needs this service, what the job involves, common signs, pricing cues (if you show them), and a clear next step.
- On-page SEO: headings, URL recommendation, and internal-link plan to other site pages.
- Conversion elements: click-to-call button on mobile, visible phone number, and estimate form optimized for that job.
- Publishing support: we can deliver the ready-to-publish files or publish directly for a hands-free option.

Tracking and reporting — what to insist on first

- Track phone calls and form submissions before judging whether the plan is working. If you haven’t set call tracking, you’ll confuse visits with leads.
- Reporting should tie pages and searches to actual calls, forms, and booked jobs—not just rankings.

If you want the operational checklist, here it is:

- Confirm phone call tracking for main business number.
- Confirm form tracking and destination URLs for estimate requests.
- Share Google Search Console and Google Analytics access.
- Complete a short list of one to three priority services you want more of.
- Approve the first service page outline.

How we avoid common pitfalls

- No fixed monthly page quota: production follows opportunity, not a public article quota.
- No fake location pages: location pages are only created for areas you truly serve.
- No vague reporting: monthly reports should show which queries produced calls and whether those calls matched the services and service areas.

Questions a Houston owner should ask before signing up

- Do you track calls and forms as conversions?
- Which competitors are you benchmarking against and why?
- Will you publish for me or deliver for my team to upload?
- What happens after the initial 90-day phase?
