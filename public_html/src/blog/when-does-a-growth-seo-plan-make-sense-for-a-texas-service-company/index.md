## Short answer
A growth SEO plan that includes a larger content push—like a 15-article program—makes sense when your business can clearly use more searchable pages, has the capacity to handle more qualified inquiries, and already has basic tracking and local signals in place. If you’re still unsure, use the quick checklist and decision framework below to see if this is the right next step.

![Blog featured image](cover.png)


## Why a 15-article plan is different than a small package
Russell Digital’s Home Service Search Growth System starts by prioritizing the highest-value pages based on competitor and keyword gaps, then tracks calls and estimate requests to see what actually produces leads. A small package (for example, the 3–5 article structure discussed on the site) focuses on the single biggest opportunities first. A 15-article push is a different commitment: it’s broader, intended to build coverage across more services and nearby service areas at once.

Use a larger plan when your market breadth or service mix requires multiple focused pages rather than a handful of high-priority wins.

## Decision framework: Is a 15-article growth plan right for you?
Follow these steps in order.

1. Business capacity check

- Can your crew handle a sustained increase in estimate requests or booked jobs? If not, fix capacity first.

2. Tracking and measurement

- Are calls, forms, and estimate requests tracked so you can see which pages produce real leads? If not, set conversion tracking before you publish more pages. Russell Digital sets conversion tracking during the initial 90-day phase of engagements.

3. Market opportunity

- Do competitor and keyword gaps show multiple service or location opportunities you can exploit? Ask for a Free Competitor & Keyword Report to confirm.

4. Page readiness

- Do you already have strong money pages (service pages that clearly explain the job and next step)? If your site still needs foundational rewrites, a big content rollout will underperform.

5. Territory clarity

- Are the service areas you want to target realistic? Avoid mass-producing location pages for places you can’t reliably serve.

If you answered yes to 3 of these 5 items (and especially yes to 1–3), a 15-article plan can be an efficient way to generate coverage and test which topics produce calls.

## Practical checklist before you commit to 15 articles

- **Claimed Google Business Profile:** Profile is claimed and has accurate NAP, hours, and primary category.
- **Conversion tracking in place:** Phone calls, booking links, and contact forms recorded and visible in reports.
- **Priority services identified:** You know which 3–5 services generate the most revenue and which 10–12 support or location pages are needed to capture related searches.
- **Competitor gap data:** A competitor and keyword review shows specific pages competitors rank for that you do not.
- **Publishing plan:** Decide whether you want the agency to publish for you or deliver ready-to-upload files.
- **90-day commitment accepted:** Understand the first 90 days are about tracking, research, and priority pages—not immediate rankings.

## How a 15-article plan typically gets used (sample deliverable)
Below is a transparent sample of how a larger monthly content commitment might be organized. It’s an example—actual priorities come from the competitor and keyword gaps found during the initial review.

Month 1 — Foundations and priority service pages

- Publication: 3 service pages targeting highest-value jobs you want more of.
- Tracking: Set up call and form tracking tied to those pages.
- Research: Competitor gap analysis expanded to identify the next 12 target topics.

Month 2 — Location and supportive content

- Publication: 4 focused location pages or tightly targeted service-area pages for the neighborhoods you actually serve.
- Internal links: Link new pages to the priority money pages and map service-area coverage.
- Report: Show which pages produced calls and which searches led to those pages.

Month 3 — Supporting articles and conversion improvements

- Publication: 4 articles addressing common pre-sale questions and high-intent queries (cost, timelines, emergency steps).
- Conversion: Add clear call buttons, estimate-request forms, or booking links on underperforming pages.
- Testing: Small headline and CTA tests on top pages.

Month 4 — Fill remaining gaps and iterate

- Publication: 4 remaining pieces focusing on secondary services or weakly covered areas from the gap analysis.
- Adjust: Pause topics that generated no calls and reallocate to the next best opportunities.

This sample follows the same logic Russell Digital uses: research before production, tracking before judging results, and prioritization by opportunity rather than a fixed monthly page quota. ## When a smaller package is a better fit
Choose fewer articles when any of these apply:

- You lack basic call/form tracking.
- You need major rewrites of existing money pages first.
- Your service area is narrow and one or two high-value pages will capture most local demand.

A small, focused 3–5 article approach often gives faster clarity on which searches produce booked jobs and helps you avoid wasted effort.

## What to measure while a 15-article plan runs
Focus on the lead path, not just traffic.

- Which searches sent visitors to which new pages? Use the competitor report to map queries to pages.
- Which pages produced phone calls, estimate requests, or booked jobs? Track and attribute at the page level.
- Are the inquiries in your service area and for services you actually perform? Stop spending on pages that attract irrelevant questions.

Russell Digital emphasizes call and estimate-request tracking as the primary yardstick during the early months of any engagement.
