Why this matters

If your Google Business Profile (GBP) is inconsistent, out of date, or neglected, you lose phone calls and estimate requests to competitors who look current. For Houston service companies—HVAC, plumbing, electrical, roofing, and similar trades—the profile is often the first place a customer finds your phone number, hours, and photos. It should be treated as an ongoing part of local SEO, not a one-time setup.

What ongoing GBP management actually looks like

GBP management is a set of recurring tasks that keep the profile aligned with the website, support local searches, and prompt action (call, message, request estimate). The basics you need to do regularly:

- **Business info:** Keep name, address, phone, hours, and service area identical to the website.
- **Primary and secondary categories:** Choose categories that match the services you actually provide and review them quarterly.
- **Services list:** Maintain a clear set of service items (for example, “AC repair,” “water heater replacement,” “roof leak repair”) and make sure they mirror your service pages.
- **Posts and updates:** Publish short posts about specials, seasonal tips, or recent jobs. Use posts to show timely availability for emergency work.
- **Photos:** Add project, team, and location photos on a schedule so the profile looks active.
- **Review operations:** Request reviews, acknowledge them, and respond to negative feedback with next steps.

A practical checklist you can use today

Daily

- Check for new messages and respond within business policy.

Weekly

- Publish one GBP post or update (status, limited-time offer, or job highlight).
- Upload at least one recent photo from a completed job.

Monthly

- Audit NAP (name, address, phone) vs. website and other listings. Fix mismatches.
- Review categories and services. Update to reflect work you want more of.
- Export recent reviews and note patterns (mentions of punctuality, price, workmanship).

Quarterly

- Refresh service list to match any new service pages or priority jobs from your SEO plan.
- Compare GBP insights (calls, direction requests, photo views) to website call-tracking.
- Update hours for seasonality and check holiday schedules.

Decision framework: DIY vs. managed GBP work

Use these three checks in order. Do only what follows each step.

1. Business capacity check

- Can your crew handle a steady flow of new estimate requests? If not, fix capacity before pushing visibility.

2. Tracking and measurement

- Are calls and form submissions tracked so you can tell which leads started on Google Maps? If no, stop and set up call tracking first.

3. Priority and opportunity

- Do competitors have more complete profiles or recent posts and photos? If yes, prioritize a managed GBP checklist and align it with your highest-value services.

If you fail any step, correct it before investing in more GBP activity. This mirrors the same decision framework Russell Digital uses when deciding whether a larger SEO or content plan makes sense for a Houston service company.

A transparent 90-day sample deliverable for GBP + local SEO alignment

This is a sample sequence you can use as a checklist or deliverable when hiring a partner. It focuses on measurable actions rather than vague promises.

Days 1–14 — Foundations

- Claim and verify the GBP.
- Match NAP to the website. Confirm phone numbers match tracking numbers where used.
- Set primary and secondary categories to reflect the main services.
- Publish an initial GBP post announcing availability and one recent job photo.
- Configure messaging, booking link, and service list entries.

Days 15–45 — Measurement and content

- Set up call and form tracking so every GBP-origin call is visible in reports.
- Publish weekly GBP posts (3 total) that link to the matching service page on your website.
- Upload 6–10 project and team photos with descriptive captions.

Days 46–90 — Optimization and reporting

- Review GBP insights and compare calls/direction requests to website conversions.
- Adjust services list and categories based on which services produced calls.
- Implement a review request process and reply template for common review types.
- Deliver a simple report showing: profile views, calls from GBP, messages, and the list of changes made.

What to measure (keep it simple)

- Number of calls from the GBP (tracked).
- Number of messages and booking clicks.
- Direction requests (as an indicator of in-area demand).
- Review count and average rating.
- Photo views and post engagement.

Tie each metric back to the real goal: more qualified phone calls and estimate requests. Russell Digital emphasizes tracking calls and forms before judging whether work produced real business outcomes.

Common profile mistakes that kill leads

- Inconsistent phone or hours between GBP and website.
- Wrong primary category that attracts unrelated searches.
- No recent photos or posts, which makes the business look inactive.
- Not tracking calls, so you can’t tell whether increased visibility actually produces leads.

How GBP work fits into a broader local plan

GBP is one local signal among several. It should be coordinated with service pages, location pages, and call tracking so the traffic coming from Maps converts into estimates or booked jobs. Use paid search when you need faster testing or immediate lead volume, but only after the page the GBP links to matches the searcher’s intent.
