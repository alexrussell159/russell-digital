## Short answer — start with the channel that matches your timeline and urgency
If you need booked jobs or estimate requests this week, start with Google Ads. If you can wait for steady, compounding lead flow and want lower ongoing cost-per-lead long term, start with SEO. Many Houston service businesses use both: Ads for immediate demand and SEO to build a reliable organic channel.

This article helps you decide using four simple criteria: timeline, urgency, margins, and search demand. It includes a short checklist, a 90‑day SEO sample deliverable, a 30‑day Google Ads sample deliverable, and what to measure first.

## How to read the four criteria that actually matter
- Timeline — How quickly do you need leads? SEO builds momentum over months. Russell Digital’s Home Service Search Growth System begins with an initial 90‑day phase to set tracking and priority pages, then works month to month. See pricing and the 90‑day start at [pricing](/pricing/).
- Urgency — Is this a short-term hiring push, or an ongoing need? Short-term surges (seasonal offers, emergency campaigns) favor paid search.
- Margins — Can you profitably pay for a click or a lead? Higher-margin service lines tolerate Ads while SEO ramps.
- Search demand and intent — Some searches are high-intent ("emergency AC repair Houston") and convert well from ads and focused landing pages. Other searches require broader local authority and multiple service pages to win organic clicks.

These four criteria should guide whether you begin with Ads, SEO, or both together.

## A quick decision checklist (use this now)
- **Timeline:** Need leads within 30 days? Choose Ads or a combined Ads+SEO push.
- **Urgency:** Is your schedule already booked for weeks? Use Ads to smooth demand spikes.
- **Margins:** Can the job absorb ad spend while you wait for SEO? If yes, Ads are reasonable.
- **Tracking:** Do you track calls and forms now? If not, stop. Tracking is required before you judge either channel. Russell Digital ties reporting to calls and estimate requests in every plan; see the system on [services](/services/).
- **Local signals:** Is your Google Business Profile claimed and accurate? If not, fix it before scaling either channel. Read about GBP tactics on [google-business-profile-management-for-houston-service-companies](/blog/google-business-profile-management-for-houston-service-companies/).
- **Capacity check:** Can your team handle more estimates? If not, fix capacity first.

If you answered "no" to Tracking or Google Business Profile, pause spend and fix those first. See what to check before a strategy call at [what-to-check-before-booking-an-seo-strategy-call](/blog/what-to-check-before-booking-an-seo-strategy-call/).

## Sample deliverable: What a first 90 days of SEO looks like (practical)
If you choose SEO first (or alongside Ads), Russell Digital’s public pricing describes a 90‑day engagement that prioritizes the right work.

Month 1 — Foundations - Conversion tracking for calls, forms, and estimate requests set up and tested. - Competitor and search-gap research to find the profitable searches you’re missing. - One high-priority service page published or a major homepage rewrite.

Month 2 — Priority pages - Another focused service page or location page published based on opportunity. - Internal link fixes and on-page optimization for pages that should produce calls.

Month 3 — Measurement and adjust - Review which pages produced calls and estimate requests. - Prioritize the next set of pages or local content based on real lead data.

## Sample deliverable: A 30‑day Google Ads setup for immediate leads
If time is critical, a tight paid-search plan focuses on intent and landing-page fit.

1. Campaign setup focused on high-intent search terms for the service and Houston areas you serve.
2. One dedicated landing page (or send to the high-value service page) with clear call, form, and booking options.
3. Call and form tracking linked to the ad account and reporting.
4. Negative keyword list to reduce wasted spend.
5. Two weeks of search-term review and early optimization.

Paid testing helps you learn which searches actually convert before you build a dozen organic pages for them. Russell Digital runs Ads when speed matters; see the two-channel view at the site root: [Home](/).

## When to run both Channels together
Use both when you need immediate leads and want the long-term cost benefits of organic traffic. Typical sequence:
1. Launch Ads to capture immediate demand and test which keywords convert.
2. Use Ads data to prioritize SEO pages (the queries that produced calls are the pages to build first).
3. Shift budget gradually into SEO-optimized pages as organic traffic starts to produce leads.

This is the practical mix Russell Digital describes: SEO first for foundations, Ads when speed matters. Read the channel comparison context in [houston-seo-and-google-ads-should-you-use-one-agency-for-both](/blog/houston-seo-and-google-ads-should-you-use-one-agency-for-both/).

## What to measure in the first 60–90 days
- Which search terms sent visitors that called or completed a form.
- Which landing pages produced estimate requests.
- Cost per qualified lead for Ads and an early estimate of organic lead value.
- Whether leads match your service area and job type.

If you’re not tracking calls and forms, start there. Read why tracking matters on [why-local-businesses-in-houston-should-track-calls-before-judging-seo](/blog/why-local-businesses-in-houston-should-track-calls-before-judging-seo/).
