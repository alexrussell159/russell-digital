Short answer — expect early movement in the first 8–12 weeks and the biggest value from steady work over several months. This article explains what typically happens, what you should measure, and a clear checklist you can use to decide whether to start with a smaller starter package or a broader growth push.

![Blog featured image](cover.png)


## Why there’s no one fixed timeline

SEO is not a single action you flip on. It’s several tasks that need to happen in the right order: tracking, research, page work, local signals, and then measured follow‑up. Russell Digital’s approach begins with an initial 90‑day commitment because the first three months are where you set tracking and publish the pages that should produce early results. Some movement can happen sooner. The biggest gains usually compound across months.

## What usually happens in the first 90 days (sample deliverable)

This is a practical, transparent example of the work most Houston home‑service companies should expect during the initial phase. It mirrors the public description of the first 90 days used by Russell Digital without promising specific outcomes.

- **Week 0–2: Foundations and tracking**
- **Task:** Confirm analytics and phone/form tracking. Install or improve call tracking so you can see which page or search produced each inquiry.
- **Why:** Many businesses stop early because they can’t tell whether a lead came from SEO or elsewhere.

- **Week 2–5: Competitor and keyword research**
- **Task:** Identify the profitable local searches you’re missing and which competitors are winning those calls. Prioritize the single highest‑value job and service areas you can support.
- **Why:** Research should drive pages, not a spreadsheet nobody uses.

- **Week 4–8: Publish the first priority page(s)**
- **Task:** Build or rewrite the one service page most likely to produce qualified calls (for example: AC repair, panel upgrades, drain cleaning, or roof repair depending on your trade).
- **Why:** A homepage cannot replace targeted service pages.

- **Week 8–12: Local signals and measurement**
- **Task:** Improve Google Business Profile details, gather recent photos and reviews, and connect local pages. Start reviewing which searches and pages produced calls and estimate requests.
- **Why:** Local signals and reviews help Maps visibility and push higher‑intent searchers to call.

This timeline is a sample. The exact mix changes depending on the market, competition, and whether Russell Digital publishes the pages or you do.

## What usually takes longer and why

- Building broad coverage across many services or multiple nearby cities takes months because each useful page needs time to rank and produce tracked calls.
- Competing against sites with stronger local content and signals requires steady content and local work rather than one big push.
- Trust‑building signals like accumulated reviews, steady traffic signals, and internal links compound slowly.

That’s why the public pricing page explains the system continues month‑to‑month after the initial 90 days and why the company avoids promising fixed timelines.

## A practical decision framework: starter vs. growth (how to choose)

Follow these steps in order. Do not skip the first two.

1. Business capacity check

- Can your crew handle a sustained increase in estimate requests or booked jobs? If not, fix capacity first.

2. Measurement check

- Are phone calls and form submissions being recorded reliably and tied to landing pages? If not, fix tracking before spending more.

3. Opportunity and coverage check

- If you only need one high‑value job (for example a single service you want more of), a smaller, starter approach (focusing on 3–5 high‑priority articles/pages) is sensible.
- If you need coverage across many services or service areas, choose a broader growth program that prioritizes multiple pages at once.

4. Risk and speed check

- If you need faster feedback on which keywords produce qualified calls, use paid search for testing while the organic plan runs. Russell Digital notes Google Ads is used when speed or testing matters.

## Checklist: what to insist on before you judge progress

- **Call and form tracking:** Must be in place and tied to pages.
- **Priority pages:** At least one clear service page aimed at a high‑value search should be published in the first 90 days.
- **Competitor gap research:** You should receive a clear list of the competitor pages and local searches you’re missing.
- **Google Business Profile:** Confirm it’s claimed and accurate (name, phone, service area, hours, categories).
- **Monthly reporting:** Reports must tie visibility to calls, forms, and estimate requests—not only traffic.

If those five items are missing after 90 days, pause and re‑evaluate.

## How to measure whether SEO is working for your Houston business

- Track the number of qualified phone calls and estimate requests from the pages you targeted.
- Watch lead quality, not just volume. Are the calls about the services you want to sell in your service area?
- Use the monthly report to decide what page gets prioritized next rather than chasing raw traffic numbers.

Russell Digital’s public approach emphasizes turning visibility into phone calls and estimate requests and avoiding bloated dashboards.

## When to consider expanding into a growth plan

- You’re consistently seeing tracked calls from the first priority pages.
- Your team can handle more estimate requests.
- You want coverage across more services or nearby cities.

A larger growth push makes sense only when tracking and capacity are ready.

- Can you review my current call/form tracking quickly?
- Which single service page should we publish first to test demand?
- What competitor pages are winning the calls we want to capture?
- How does the proposed plan tie to calls and estimate requests, not just rankings?
