## Short answer: what a good call should do
A free SEO strategy call should give you a clear short list of what to fix first and how those fixes connect to real leads — not a vague audit or a long sales pitch.

For Houston service businesses, the call should show which services and local searches matter, confirm whether calls and forms are being tracked, and leave you with the first concrete steps you can take in the next 30–90 days.

![Blog featured image](cover.png)


## Before the call: quick prep checklist
Do these six things so the call is practical and focused:

- **Know your top 1–3 business priorities:** Which service do you want more of (for example, AC repair, drain cleaning, roof repair)?
- **Confirm tracking access:** Can you share Google Search Console and Google Analytics if asked?
- **List how customers contact you now:** Phone, forms, booking links, or walk‑ins?
- **Have basic site access info ready:** CMS login or who publishes your site.
- **Bring two or three competitor sites you want to compare:** Helpful for real examples on the call.
- **Confirm your Google Business Profile is claimed or who manages it:** That usually matters for local visibility.

If you can’t gather all of these, the call can still be useful — but having them lets the advisor get into practical recommendations faster. See a short checklist version on [What to check before booking an SEO strategy call](/blog/what-to-check-before-booking-an-seo-strategy-call/).

## What the call should actually cover
A good 20–30 minute strategy call for a Houston service business should cover these items and end with a clear next step:

- **Current lead sources:** Which pages, searches, or ads are producing calls and estimate requests now.
- **Tracking gaps:** Whether calls, forms, and estimate requests are being recorded reliably.
- **Service priorities:** Which specific services create the most revenue and should get focused pages first.
- **Competitive gaps:** Where competitors are winning the calls you want and which pages you’re missing.
- **First 90‑day plan:** The exact pages, tracking fixes, or quick publishes that should come first.
- **Measurement plan:** How you’ll see whether the changes actually produce more qualified calls and estimate requests.

This matches the Home Service Search Growth System approach: find the missed searches, create the pages needed to compete, and track whether visibility turns into calls and estimate requests. See the system overview on [services](/services/).

## A simple decision framework to use on the call
Use this order when deciding whether to move forward and what level of work you need.

1. **Capacity check** — Can your team handle more estimate requests or booked jobs? If not, fix capacity before chasing more traffic.
2. **Tracking and data** — Can you track which page or search generated a call or form? If not, set tracking first.
3. **Service coverage** — Do you have a focused page for the single highest‑value local service you want more of? If not, build that page first.
4. **Competitive gap** — Are competitors showing up for the profitable searches you want? If so, prioritize the pages they use.
5. **Budget fit** — Is a small 3–5 article package enough, or does the market need a broader 15‑article growth plan? Russell Digital explains what different packages look like on the [pricing](/pricing/) page.

Decide in that order. Quantity of content matters only after you’ve fixed tracking, prioritized the money pages, and confirmed you can actually handle more work.

## A transparent sample deliverable: the first 90 days
Here is a realistic set of deliverables you can expect from a focused 90‑day start. It’s practical, not a promise of rankings or leads.

- **Initial work (week 1–2):**
- Competitor & keyword review focused on the Houston service searches you care about.
- Conversion setup for relevant calls, forms, and estimate requests so you can measure leads.
- Short list of 1–3 high‑priority pages to publish or rewrite first.

- **Month 1:**
- Publish 1 primary service page or major rewrite that targets your single highest‑value job.
- Fine‑tune conversion points on that page (phone prominence, clear estimate request form).
- Start tracking calls and form submissions tied to that page.

- **Month 2:**
- Publish 1–2 supporting pages (location pages, related services, or an article that answers a common hire question).
- Begin internal linking from the new pages to the primary money page.
- Review early call and form data; remove obvious traffic/keyword mismatches.

- **Month 3:**
- Adjust the pages and calls based on which searches produced qualified leads.
- Recommend next pages and whether a broader growth plan makes sense.
- Deliver a monthly report that ties visibility changes to calls and estimate requests.

This mirrors the initial 90‑day emphasis used in the Home Service Search Growth System and the description on the [pricing](/pricing/) and [services](/services/) pages. Public pricing starts at $1,500/month and the engagement commonly begins with an initial 90‑day phase.

## Questions to ask during the call
Bring these to keep the conversation practical:

- Which single service should we prioritize first?
- Can you show me the last 30 days of calls or form submissions (even a screenshot)?
- Do you have access to Search Console or Analytics?
- Who publishes changes to your website now?
- Who manages your Google Business Profile and reviews?
- How many estimate requests per week can your team realistically handle?

If those answers are clear, the call can move past discovery and into the first fixes.

## How to judge the call’s value
A good strategy call ends with one or more of these outcomes:

- A prioritized short list of pages or fixes you can action in 30–90 days.
- A plan to add or fix conversion tracking so leads are measurable.
- A clear next‑step recommendation: small package, 90‑day start, or follow‑up audit.

If the call only repeats generalities, ask for concrete next steps tied to calls and estimate requests.
