## The short answer
If your Houston website gets traffic but not calls or estimate requests, the problem is usually tracking, page fit, or missing service pages — not some mysterious Google penalty. Fix those three things first and you’ll know whether SEO is actually producing qualified leads.

This piece is practical: a decision framework to choose the right level of SEO, a hands-on checklist you can use today, and a sample 90-day deliverable built from the exact Russell Digital approach (the initial 90-day commitment, competitor research, and call/form tracking you see on our site).

## Start with the goal: calls, forms, and booked jobs
Too many SEO reports focus on impressions and rankings. Those matter only insofar as they produce phone calls, estimate requests, or booked work. Ask this first: which searches should bring you a qualified call? The answer defines the pages you need, the tracking you set up, and whether you need paid search while waiting for organic traction.

- If you want more same-day repairs, target emergency and repair pages.
- If you need more replacement jobs, target service and project pages that explain the process and pricing ranges.
- If you want commercial leads, prioritize location pages and case studies for businesses.

## A practical checklist that actually produces calls and estimate requests
Use this on your site or hand it to your contractor. Each item is actionable and connected to a lead outcome.

- **Track calls and forms:** Ensure phone calls and form submissions are recorded and attributed to the page and search that produced them. )
- **Match page to intent:** Create one page per clear job you want more of (example: “AC repair,” “panel upgrades,” “roof leak repair”). A homepage cannot replace focused service pages.
- **Localize where it matters:** Include the service area only when you actually serve it. Use location details on relevant service pages rather than dozens of thin city pages.
- **Clear next step:** Each service page must have one obvious action: call, request estimate, or booking link. Don’t force visitors to search the site for the phone number.
- **Competitor gap research:** Identify the specific queries and pages competitors rank for and which ones you don’t. Prioritize pages that competitors use to win calls.
- **Publish and test:** Publish the page, run for a few weeks, then check whether it produced calls or form leads. If not, revise the headline, offer, and CTA.
- **Measure lead quality:** Track whether inquiries match your service area and the job types you accept. Low-quality volume is not a win.

## A transparent measurement playbook
If you take only one thing from this article, it’s this: measure calls and form completions before judging whether SEO is working.

- Record: Which search brought the visitor in.
- Attribute: Which page they landed on.
- Convert: Did they call or fill the estimate form?
- Qualify: Was the inquiry for a service and area you actually serve?
- Decide: Keep, revise, or pause the page based on lead quality.
