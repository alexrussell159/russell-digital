## Quick answer
Choose the option that fits your capacity, timeline, and who will be accountable for turning search visibility into calls and estimate requests. There is no universal winner. A consultant can be tightly accountable and lower-cost for one-off fixes or strategic guidance. An agency can provide capacity, execution, and ongoing publishing when you need hands-off work at scale.

This article walks through the tradeoffs—accountability, capacity, specialization, and cost—then gives a simple decision checklist, a transparent methodology you can expect, and a sample 90‑day deliverable so you know what each option should actually produce.

## Decision framework (use this simple matrix)
Score each row 1–5 for your business (1 = low fit for the option, 5 = high fit). Add the columns for Consultant and Agency and compare totals.

- Urgency: Do you need leads in weeks or can you wait months?
- Execution capacity: Can your team publish content and implement tracking, or do you need hands-free publishing?
- Budget predictability: Do you prefer pay-as-you-go or a stable monthly plan starting at $1,500/month?
- Single point of contact: Do you want one expert responsible or a small team with role coverage?
- Scope of work: Audit/strategy only, or strategy + writing + publishing + tracking + ads?

How to read it:

- If Urgency = 5 and Scope = broad, Agency likely scores higher.
- If Execution capacity = 5 (your team handles publishing) and Scope = narrow, Consultant may be a better fit.

## Quick checklist: When to pick a consultant
- You need a one-off audit, technical fixes, or a clear strategy session.
- You have internal capacity to implement pages and tracking.
- You want tight, direct accountability from one person.
- Your budget favors project work over an ongoing monthly retainer.

## Quick checklist: When to pick an agency
- You want hands-free publishing and ongoing content produced.
- You need call/form tracking tied to reporting and decisions.
- You plan to scale pages across services and service areas.
- You value role coverage so work doesn’t stall when someone is unavailable.
