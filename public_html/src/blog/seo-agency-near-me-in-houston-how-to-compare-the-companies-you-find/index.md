## Quick checklist: What to check before you call an agency

- **Proof of process:** Can they describe a 90-day starting plan that sets tracking, finds missed opportunities, and publishes prioritized pages? Ask them to walk you through it.
- **Tracking focus:** Do they track phone calls, form submissions, and estimate requests — and can they tie those back to the page and search term? If they can’t, treat their reports as guesses.
- **Deliverables tied to searches:** Do they build service pages, local pages, or rewrites based on competitor gaps, not a promised page quota? Look for examples.
- **Publishing support:** Will they publish for you or hand you files? If you want hands-free work, confirm publishing is included. Look for clear milestones instead.
- **Territory protection:** Do they work with only one direct competitor in your agreed service category and area?
- **Pricing clarity:** Do they give a starting price and explain what changes the final scope? Ask what their starting monthly price includes.

If the agency can’t answer these plainly, you’ll lose time and money testing them in the market.

## A simple decision framework you can use on the first call

1. Business capacity check

- Can your team handle more estimate requests or booked jobs? If not, pause hiring an agency until you fix capacity.

2. Measurement check

- Can they show how they set up call and form tracking? If they can’t map leads back to pages and searches, stop.

3. Opportunity check

- Did they run competitor and keyword research focused on profitable local searches? Ask for a short sample list of missed searches.

4. Prioritization check

- Do they propose priorities by opportunity (highest-value jobs first) rather than by a fixed monthly page count?

5. Commitment and terms

- What’s the initial commitment? What happens after the first phase? Make sure month-to-month continuation and territory protection are clear.

If the answer to steps 2 or 3 is no, treat the proposal as exploratory, not execution-ready.

## What a useful 90-day starter plan actually does (sample deliverable)

Below is a transparent sample you can ask a Houston agency to show as proof. These items are consistent with practical local SEO work for service businesses.

Month 0–1 — Tracking and research

- **Conversion tracking:** Set up phone-call tracking, form tracking, and estimate-request tracking tied to pages.
- **Competitor & keyword review:** Produce a short report showing the competitors that rank for the profitable local searches you want and the searches you’re missing.
- **Priority list:** Deliver 3–5 prioritized page or rewrite recommendations based on the highest-value services and areas.

Month 2 — High-value page delivery

- **Publication:** Publish or deliver the single highest-value service page or major rewrite that targets the job you want more of.
- **On-page setup:** Ensure the page clearly explains the job, the service area, and next steps (call or request estimate).

Month 3 — Test, measure, and adjust

- **Measurement review:** Report which searches and pages produced calls and forms; recommend the next 3–5 pages based on real lead data.
- **Hands-off options:** If you want publishing handled for you, confirm the agency has direct publishing support.

This is a practical scope. It doesn’t promise rankings. It ties work to the calls and estimate requests that matter.

## Questions to ask that reveal capability (use these on a strategy call)

- How will you record phone calls and attribute them to a specific page or search?
- Which service pages would you build first for my business, and why?
- Will you publish the pages for me or hand off files? If you publish, what access do you need?
- Do you limit work to one direct competitor per service and territory?
- What exactly is included in your initial 90-day phase?
- How do you decide whether to use Google Ads for speed or stick with SEO?

Bring these to any call and compare the answers from each agency you’re vetting.

## Red flags to walk away from

- **Vague tracking answers:** If they can’t show how calls and forms are tracked and attributed, step back.
- **Commodity page quotas:** If the proposal focuses on a fixed number of pages without showing opportunity-based priorities, be cautious.
- **No sample work:** If they can’t show a sample competitor report or a 90-day action plan, ask for it in writing.

## How to compare price vs. scope — a practical rule

- Price matters less than what is measured. A lower monthly fee that doesn’t set up tracking or build priority pages will waste time.
- Look for an initial starter phase that sets tracking, finds missed searches, and publishes the first high-value page before a longer commitment.
- Confirm month-to-month continuation after an initial period so you can judge results and adjust.
