## The decision framework: nine checks to separate claims from proof
Use these checks in order. They are designed so you can reasonably verify each claim during a short call or by reviewing the vendor’s sample deliverables.

1. Business capacity check

- Can your team handle more estimates and booked jobs? If not, fix capacity before paying for new leads.

2. Tracking and measurement

- Ask whether the agency will set up call and form tracking tied to specific pages and search sources. If they can’t show how they track which search produced a call, the work will be hard to evaluate. Read a short primer on what to check before you book a call: [What to check before booking an SEO strategy call](/blog/what-to-check-before-booking-an-seo-strategy-call/).

3. Page-level focus (not vague tasks)

- The vendor should prioritize service pages and local pages for the specific jobs you want more of. A homepage cannot do the job of focused pages. See why focused service pages matter: [/blog/how-focused-service-pages-help-local-businesses-attract-better-leads/](/blog/how-focused-service-pages-help-local-businesses-attract-better-leads/).

4. Initial commitment and quick validation

- A useful engagement usually begins with a short, focused phase that sets tracking, finds missed searches, and delivers a few high-value pages. 5. Pricing transparency and scope

- Look for clear starting price ranges and what’s included. If the vendor refuses to outline the first 90 days of work and deliverables, beware. Russell Digital lists a public starting price and describes the first 90 days on the [pricing page](/pricing/).

## A short scoring checklist you can use on sales calls

Give Yes = 1, No = 0. Total score helps you prioritize interviews.

- **Business capacity confirmed:** Can you handle increased lead flow? Yes/No
- **Conversion tracking offered:** Will they set up call + form tracking? Yes/No
- **Service-page plan:** Do they propose specific service pages, not just a content quota? Yes/No
- **Initial 90-day plan:** Do they outline the first 90 days of measurable work? Yes/No
- **Published deliverables:** Will they publish pages for you if needed? Yes/No
- **Territory policy:** Do they limit direct competitors per territory? Yes/No
- **Transparent pricing:** Is there a clear starting price and what it includes? Yes/No
- **Outcome reporting:** Will reports tie visibility to calls/estimates? Yes/No

A vendor that scores poorly on tracking, page focus, or the 90‑day plan is unlikely to be worth the monthly spend.

## Questions to ask on the final call

- Show me an example 90‑day plan for a business in my industry.
- Who will own conversion tracking and where will I see the call/form data?
- Which specific service pages would you create first and why?
- Do you publish for clients or only deliver copy? If you publish, how fast?
- What happens if results don’t move in the first 90 days? Is the next step clear and measurable?

If those answers are vague, the relationship will be too.
