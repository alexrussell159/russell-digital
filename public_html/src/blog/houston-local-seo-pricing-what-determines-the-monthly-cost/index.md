## The real cost drivers (and what to check first)
- **Market competition:** High-demand neighborhoods and big-city searches require more work to compete. Check who ranks for the exact services you sell.
- **Which services you want to push:** Emergency repairs, replacements, or specialty installs all attract different searches. Building separate pages for each important service costs more than patching one homepage.
- **Content and publishing needs:** Do you need the agency to write and publish pages, or will you upload drafts? Hands-free publishing increases monthly scope because the agency does the work end-to-end. Russell Digital offers optional publishing support as part of the Home Service Search Growth System. See [/services/](/services/).
- **Technical fixes and site speed:** Slow sites or older CMS setups often need technical work before new pages can rank. That work is usually billed as part of the initial plan.
- **Local signals and Google Business Profile work:** Optimizing your Google Business Profile and keeping categories, photos, and reviews aligned takes ongoing attention for local visibility.
- **Tracking and reporting:** Tracking calls, forms, and estimate requests is non-negotiable. If tracking is missing, the first phase will include setup, which affects initial costs. Russell Digital prioritizes conversion tracking as a baseline.
- **Territory and exclusivity:** Some vendors limit the number of direct competitors they work with in a territory; that can influence price and availability of the agency’s time.
- **Ad-related work:** If you combine SEO with Google Ads for speed, expect additional monthly management fees tied to campaign complexity.

## A simple decision framework to estimate what you’ll pay
Use this short framework to decide whether the public starting price is realistic for your business.

1. Business readiness — do you have crews, capacity, and processes to handle more calls and estimates? If no, a lower-cost, lighter-scope plan may be appropriate.
2. Website condition — is the site modern, mobile-friendly, and fast? If not, expect initial technical work.
3. Page needs — do you already have focused service pages for each profitable job? If not, building pages raises scope.
4. Publishing preference — do you want the agency to publish or provide deliverables for your team to upload? Hands-free publishing increases monthly cost but reduces your workload.
5. Tracking status — are call tracking and form tracking already in place? If not, include tracking setup in the initial 90 days.
6. Competition level — search a few high-intent queries in Houston for your services. If competitors have strong, targetted pages, expect more initial work.
7. Timeline — do you need leads quickly? If yes, add Google Ads (additional fee) rather than relying only on organic SEO.

If you answered “no” to steps 2–4 or “yes” to 6–7, plan on a scope larger than the public starting price.

## A transparent 90-day sample deliverable (what you should expect first)
The first 90 days set the foundation. A clear, practical deliverable looks like this:

- **Free Competitor & Keyword Report** that shows where competitors win and which searches you’re missing.
- **Tracking setup** for calls, forms, and estimate requests so reporting ties directly to business outcomes.
- **Priority list of service and location pages** prioritized by estimated lead value (not a fixed page quota).
- **One or more published deliverables or delivered drafts** (service pages, location pages, or supporting articles) depending on whether you choose hands-free publishing.
- **Technical checklist and fixes** for site speed, mobile display, or CMS issues discovered during the audit.
- **Weekly or biweekly check-ins** that explain progress and next priorities in plain language.
- **Clear month‑to‑month plan** after 90 days based on measured results and remaining opportunity.

## Budget decisions business owners should make now
- Prioritize the services that bring the highest revenue per job.
- Decide how much of the work you want the agency to publish for you.
- Commit to tracking calls and forms before judging ROI.
- Choose a timeline: faster results often mean combining SEO with paid search.

## Questions to ask any SEO proposal for Houston
- What exactly happens in the first 90 days?
- Will you set up call and form tracking? How will I see which searches produce calls?
- Do you publish pages or deliver editable drafts? What does hands-free publishing include?
- How do you prioritize which pages to build first?
- Are there any territory protections or competitor rules?
