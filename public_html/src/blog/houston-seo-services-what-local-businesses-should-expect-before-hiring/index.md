## Quick Answer
A serious Houston SEO engagement should include:

- an early competitor and keyword review that shows missed service searches
- conversion tracking for calls, forms, and estimate requests
- a clear first-90-day plan with prioritized page work
- hands‑on execution or deliverables you can publish yourself
- monthly reporting centered on inquiries and visible work completed
- direct, owner-led communication and an explicit territory policy

## What to expect in month one
Start by fixing measurement and blocking issues. Do this exact order: 

1. Confirm tracking: Search Console, Analytics, and call/form tracking.
2. Check crawl and indexing issues: find pages Google can’t or won’t index.
3. Review main service pages: identify gaps vs. competitors for profitable searches.
4. Fix the clearest technical or conversion blockers.
5. Improve one page tied to revenue and publish or hand off for publishing.

This order answers the single useful question: what’s stopping you from getting more qualified calls right now?

## The decision framework before you sign

- Fit: Are you an established, independently owned home-service company in Houston with capacity for more calls? Russell Digital focuses on companies like HVAC, plumbing, roofing, and similar trades.
- Scope: Ask for a short written 90-day plan that lists specific pages, tracking work, and one conversion lift to start.
- Reporting promise: Reporting should be tied to phone calls, forms, and estimate requests — not just visits.
- Territory: Confirm whether the agency limits direct competitors in the same service category and agreed territory.
- Cost clarity: Know the public starting price and what affects final scope. Russell Digital publishes a public starting price and an initial 90-day engagement before month-to-month work.

If an agency can’t answer those five items plainly, pause.

## A sample deliverable: Month 1 (what you should receive)

- Competitor & keyword report showing the specific service and location searches you are missing.
- Tracking checklist completed: Search Console, Analytics, and lead-tracking for calls/forms.
- Prioritized page list: 3–6 pages ranked by expected lead impact.
- One published page improvement or a finished draft ready for hands-free publishing.
- A simple month-end report showing what was done and which lead channels were measured (calls, forms, estimate requests).

That package turns vague promises into testable work.

## What good reporting looks like
Reports should be concise and practical. Expect these sections every month: 

- What we did this month: list of pages updated, technical fixes, and published content.
- What changed: calls, forms, estimate requests attributable to site work (not just raw sessions).
- Visibility notes: any meaningful ranking or impression changes worth mentioning.
- Next 30 days: concrete priorities and one measurable test (example: publish a rewritten service page and test a new call-tracking configuration).

Avoid bloated dashboards. The point is to connect effort to inquiries.

## Communication and cadence
A reasonable cadence keeps owners informed without micromanagement: 

- A short kickoff to confirm access and priorities.
- Weekly or biweekly asynchronous updates (email or project notes) for work-in-progress.
- One monthly review call to walk through the report and set the next 30 days.
- Clear ownership: who publishes, who tests forms, and who tracks calls.

Russell Digital emphasizes direct, owner-led communication and wants clients to understand the plan rather than be handed off into a black box.

## Red flags to watch for

- Soft scope: no written 90-day plan or vague ‘ongoing SEO’ language.
- No access requests: if they don’t ask for Search Console, Analytics, or Google Business Profile access, they’re guessing.
- Vanity reporting: lots of traffic graphs but no connection to calls or estimates.
- Oblivious to service pages: prioritizing generic blog quotas over pages that match buyer intent.

If you see one or more of these, ask for a clearer deliverable list before you pay.
