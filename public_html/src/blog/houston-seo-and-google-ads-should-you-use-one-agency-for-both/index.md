## Short answer
If you want simpler coordination and fewer handoffs, one agency can work. But channel strategy and reporting should stay separate so you can tell which searches, pages, and campaigns actually produce calls and estimate requests.

This article helps Houston service businesses decide whether a single partner for SEO and Google Ads is the right choice. It explains where combined work helps, where separation matters, and gives a decision framework, checklist, and a sample 90-day deliverable you can use in conversations with any agency.

## Why some Houston businesses prefer one agency for both
Coordination is the real benefit. When SEO and Google Ads are run by the same team you get:

- **Shared tracking and attribution:** One tag plan, consistent call and form tracking, and matching UTM conventions mean fewer gaps when you compare paid and organic leads.
- **Aligned landing pages:** Ads can send traffic to the same service pages the SEO team is building, so landing-page relevance improves faster.
- **Faster testing:** Paid search can test which keywords and page messaging produce calls before you commit to a larger organic content push.
- **Single point of contact:** Less back-and-forth when the teams need changes on pages, tracking, or ad copy.

## Where strategy and reporting must remain separate
Even when one agency handles both channels, keep these three things distinct:

- **Channel strategy:** SEO is about building pages and local signals for long-term visibility. Google Ads is about bidding, match types, and budget to capture immediate intent. The recommendations should be tailored to those differences.
- **Performance reporting:** Reports should show paid and organic metrics separately so you can see which searches and pages actually create calls, estimate requests, or booked jobs. Don’t let combined metrics hide tradeoffs.
- **Decision ownership:** Use paid search to test keywords and page messaging, but treat organic work as a prioritized content plan based on measured lead value—not a one-to-one copy of ad behavior.

1. **Check tracking readiness** — Do you reliably track calls and forms now? If not, fix tracking first. If calls aren’t tracked correctly, you can’t compare channels. 2. **Confirm capacity to act on leads** — If your crew can’t handle more estimate requests, stop. No reporting will help if you can’t service the demand.

## Quick checklist to use in a sales call or RFP

- **Tracking:** Are phone calls, form submissions, and estimate requests tracked and attributed to source and landing page?
- **90-day starter:** Does the vendor propose an initial 90-day plan that sets tracking, runs tests, and prioritizes pages?
- **Separate reports:** Will paid and organic results be reported separately on the same cadence?
- **Landing-page ownership:** Who owns page edits? How fast can pages be updated after ad tests?
- **Budget transparency:** Are ads billed separately and clearly tied to lead outcomes?
- **Conflict rules:** Will the agency work with direct competitors in the same service area? )

Use this list during a call. If the agency says yes to all, you can work with one partner. If not, consider splitting channels until those controls exist.

## Sample 90-day deliverable (coordinated but separate execution)

Month 1 — Foundations

- **Tracking setup:** Phone call tracking, form tracking, and UTM conventions implemented across site and ad landing pages.
- **Competitor & keyword report:** Identify the high-intent searches you’re missing and the paid keywords to test. )
- **Ad test plan:** Small paid search test focused on one high-value job and one service area.
- **SEO priority page:** Publish or rewrite the single highest-value service page.

Month 2 — Test and learn

- **Paid keyword testing:** Run ad tests and capture which terms produce calls and qualified estimate requests.
- **Page revision:** Update the service page with messaging and proof points that performed best in ad tests.
- **Reporting:** Separate paid and organic reports showing calls, forms, cost per lead (ads), and page conversion rates.

Month 3 — Scale and decide

- **Scale what works:** Ramp organic work for verified high-value searches and increase ad spend on terms that produce qualified leads.
- **Plan next pages:** Use test data to prioritize the next 3–5 pages or articles.
- **Decision point:** Continue with the combined partner or split channels based on measured performance and execution speed.
