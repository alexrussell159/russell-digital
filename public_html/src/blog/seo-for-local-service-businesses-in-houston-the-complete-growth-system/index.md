## Why these five things must work together
- **Service pages:** Each high-value job needs a focused page that answers the question a customer typed and makes calling or requesting an estimate obvious.
- **Maps / Google Business Profile:** Maps visibility puts you in front of people who search "near me". The profile must match the site and show relevant categories, hours, photos, and reviews.
- **Reviews:** Recent, service-specific reviews help convert local searches into calls.
- **Supporting content:** Articles and local pages fill gaps and capture related searches without bloating the homepage.
- **Conversion & tracking:** Track which searches and pages produced the call or form. Without that, you can’t tell which pages are worth expanding.

If any one of these is weak, the whole chain breaks.

## Houston Local SEO decision framework (use this order)
1. Business capacity check
2. Tracking setup and verification
3. Service-page priority
4. Google Business Profile and review process
5. Supporting content and local pages
6. Measure, iterate, and expand

Why in this order: if you can’t handle more leads, stop. If you don’t track calls and forms, you’ll optimize the wrong pages. Only then should you build pages and content.

## Quick checklist you can run in one afternoon
- Confirm whether calls and forms are being recorded reliably. If not, fix tracking first. See details on why this matters in [Why Local Businesses in Houston Should Track Calls Before Judging SEO](/blog/why-local-businesses-in-houston-should-track-calls-before-judging-seo/).
- Identify the 1–3 services that produce the highest-margin or most frequent jobs for your crew.
- Check your homepage and confirm you are not using it as a catch-all for every service.
- Verify your Google Business Profile categories, phone, address/service area, and recent photos match the website.
- Make a simple review request process: ask customers for a short review that mentions the job completed.
- List five nearby neighborhoods or suburbs you want to serve and confirm you can realistically cover them.
- Open your competitor’s top-ranking pages for those services and note what they do differently.

## What a good service page includes (practical checklist)
- Clear headline that names the job (not only the trade).
- Short section: who needs this job and common warning signs.
- Concrete examples of the work and the neighborhoods you serve in Houston.
- A prominent phone number and an estimate-request form above the fold.
- Brief FAQ answering the most common hiring questions.
- Simple internal links to related services or the booking page.

If your homepage tries to do all of this for ten services, none of them will convert well. Build separate pages for the things you actually want more of.

## How to judge progress (the right questions to ask)
- Are more calls coming from the pages you published?
- Are the calls asking about the services you offer and within your service area?
- Did conversions improve after fixing Google Business Profile or adding reviews?
- Did one supporting article produce usable search queries that lead to calls?

If the answers are yes, you’re on the right path. If not, revisit tracking and the page-to-search fit.
